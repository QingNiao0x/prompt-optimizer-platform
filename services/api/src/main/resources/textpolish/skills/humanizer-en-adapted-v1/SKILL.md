Edit English prose for its intended reader and purpose. Read the whole passage before choosing changes.
Find the main burden: overloaded sentences, abstract noun chains, repetitive framing, unclear organization, or a register that does not fit the reader. Rewrite complete sentences or paragraphs around their actual point. Vary sentence length where it helps the argument or narrative.
Prefer direct verbs when they clarify who does what. Keep passive voice when the actor is unknown or the object deserves emphasis. Preserve established technical terminology and meaningful acronym expansions.
Cut an introduction or closing sentence only when it repeats information without adding a claim, purpose, qualification, or emphasis needed by the author. Preserve meaningful contrasts, simultaneous events, and each independent item in a list.
Use natural collocations and specific language supported by the source. Generic material may remain general. Explain an existing relationship when supported by the passage, without importing examples, outside knowledge, or personal reactions.
Preserve the author's point of view, uncertainty, humor, and personal detail. Adapt register to the requested style. Contractions can suit conversational writing when appropriate, while formal and technical prose may need precise conventional wording.
Check modality and scope in context: may, can, should, must, will, planned, confirmed, some, all, only, unless, and at least can change rights, obligations, certainty, or eligibility. Preserve their meanings even when wording changes.
Keep the author's English spelling convention when discernible. Punctuation follows the sentence and voice, with no blanket ban on dashes, adverbs, or three-part lists.
Examples illustrate methods only.
Before: "In order to help readers locate the guide, we plan to add a link to the menu."
After: "We plan to add a menu link so readers can find the guide." The plan and purpose remain.
Before: "I waited by the window until the rain stopped."
This is already natural and may stay as written.
